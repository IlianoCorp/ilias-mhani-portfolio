TIPE · Academic research project · 2025–2026

Study of the Cycle of an ABS System

From a physical braking problem to simulation and an electrical analogue.

← Back

Context

During emergency braking, wheel lock can reduce vehicle control. My TIPE explored the operating cycle of an Anti-lock Braking System and the way feedback control can keep wheel slip around a useful operating range.

The question I explored was whether a simple relay controller with hysteresis could approach an ideal continuous controller. This gave me a concrete way to connect the CPGE theme “Cycles and Loops” with a physical system.

What I did

  • Modelled wheel and vehicle behaviour to understand the relationship between braking, wheel rotation and slip.
  • Used Python to simulate and compare different braking strategies: locked wheels, relay ABS and an ideal controller.
  • Studied relay-based feedback control and how alternating braking and pressure release creates a repeating cycle.
  • Built an electrical analogy using a Schmitt trigger and RC oscillator to explore that cycle through electronics.

Tools & concepts

PythonNumerical simulationPhysical modellingControl systemsFeedback loopsElectronics

What I learned

This project helped me connect physical modelling, simulation, automatic control and experimental reasoning. Moving between a mechanical problem, Python simulations and an electrical circuit made the feedback loop feel much more concrete. I learned to compare different ways of representing the same system and to pay attention to what a simplified model can explain, as well as its limits.
